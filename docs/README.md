# OctoAcme Project Management Process Overview

OctoAcme’s project management approach is designed to move work from a validated idea to a released outcome with clear ownership, consistent communication, and measurable progress. The lifecycle begins with project initiation, where the team defines the business problem, stakeholders, goals, risks, and the initial timeline in a lightweight one-pager or charter. Once the initiative is aligned and approved, the team moves into planning, where backlog items are prioritized, estimates are assigned, milestones are mapped, and the Definition of Done is documented so delivery expectations are clear from the start.

After initiation and planning, the work enters execution, which is structured around a predictable team rhythm. Delivery teams use a project board with states such as Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done, and they coordinate daily standups, weekly status reviews, and milestone demos to surface progress, blockers, and dependencies early. Risk and stakeholder communication are tracked through a common source of truth, and escalation paths help teams resolve issues quickly when they affect schedule, business impact, or cross-functional delivery dependencies.

The operating model also emphasizes clear roles and responsibilities across the project. Product and project leaders define outcomes, priorities, and business value, while developers, QA, and supporting stakeholders contribute implementation, validation, and feedback. This cross-functional structure helps keep teams aligned and ensures that work is governed by clear acceptance criteria, disciplined review processes, and visible ownership. OctoAcme expects small, reviewable pull requests, clear issue linkage, and regular checkpoints so teams can maintain momentum while reducing avoidable handoff friction.

Quality assurance is integrated throughout delivery rather than treated as a final-stage activity. Teams are expected to write and maintain unit tests, add integration or end-to-end coverage where appropriate, and run security scans and CI checks before release. Deployment readiness includes staging validation, smoke tests, rollback planning, and post-deploy verification, while retrospectives capture lessons learned and convert them into action items with owners and due dates. This creates a continuous improvement loop that strengthens both delivery quality and project execution over time.

## Quick reference

- Initiation: validate the business need, define stakeholders, success metrics, and initial planning inputs.
- Planning: create the backlog, estimate work, define milestones, and document the Definition of Done.
- Execution: track work through the project board, hold team syncs, and manage blockers and dependencies.
- Release: validate staging, run smoke tests, deploy with rollback readiness, and confirm health post-release.
- Improvement: capture retrospective findings and close action items with clear owners.
